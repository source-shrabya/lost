# Customer discovery for the Get Out of the Building award

$500. One form per team, due Sunday, November 8, 11:59 PM. The form asks: team name, track, how many people we talked to, who (roles, not names), the three most important things we learned, what we changed or what was confirmed, optional notes upload, and who is submitting.

The same interviews give the pitch its first 30 seconds ("how you know"), so they help the main score too.

## Who to talk to

Aim for about 10 real conversations. Quality counts more than the count.

- Students in big lecture classes (5 or 6). Mix of majors, not only CS friends.
- Professors who teach large lectures (2 or 3). These are the buyers and the hardest people to reach, so they count the most.
- A TA or tutor from the Learning Center (1 or 2). They see confusion after class.
- Someone who runs classroom tech at USM, such as iTech or the teaching center (1). They know what it takes to get a tool approved.

Ask before recording: "Is it okay if I record this so I can take better notes?"

## What to ask

Ask about the last real time it happened, not what they would do. Don't pitch Lost until the end.

Students:

1. Tell me about the last time you got lost in a lecture. What class, and what was going on?
2. What did you do in that moment?
3. Did you ask a question? Why or why not?
4. What did you do after class to catch up? How long did it take?
5. What do you use now (recordings, Otter, ChatGPT, friends, office hours)? What's annoying about it?
6. At the end, show Lost for 30 seconds. Would you use it in class? What would stop you?

Professors:

1. How do you find out a part of a lecture didn't land? How long after?
2. Tell me about the last time you found out late.
3. What tools do you use in class now (iClicker, polls, Canvas)? What do you like and not like?
4. Who decides whether a new classroom tool gets used, and who pays?
5. At the end, show the professor screen. Would you want this? What worries you (privacy, distraction, accuracy)?

## Things we already learned from testing

These count as learning only if they came from real people. Rohit and Nischal tested the class flow on 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-07:

- The first test's summary broke, so students saw only the raw transcript. We switched the AI to structured output.
- Taps landed on the line before the one that lost the student, because the transcript lags behind speech. We changed taps to follow the sentence being said.
